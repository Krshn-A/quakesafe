Field notes

Know what this model is, and what it is not.

The model

What the model is

  • Frame: each floor is a mass and each storey is a spring. The stiffness matrix is tridiagonal.
  • Wall or core: a cantilever beam. Its flexibility matrix comes from the beam deflection formula, and the stiffness matrix is its inverse.
  • Static check: a simplified equivalent-lateral-force procedure in the style of ASCE 7. It uses the computed period directly and ignores the code's cap on that period. It also leaves out the long-period branch of the load, the extra lower bound on the load for sites with S1 of 0.6 g or more, and the second soft-storey rule (80% of the average of the three storeys above). In a frame + core building the soft-storey test looks at the frame alone.
  • Shake test: steady shaking at one frequency with damping proportional to stiffness.
  • Detection: a short-term over long-term average trigger, the P-to-S time gap for distance, and circle equations for the epicentre.

Limits

  • The static check is only valid for regular low and mid-rise buildings. The page warns above about 48 m.
  • The frame model is poor for tall towers. Use the wall or frame + core option, and treat results as a period comparison.
  • No yielding, cracking, twisting or collapse is modelled.
  • Floors are lumped masses, with a whole floor's mass at the roof. Against a smooth beam of the same mass a wall's first frequency comes out a few percent low: 4.8% for 20 storeys, 2.4% for 40.
  • One shaking frequency is not an earthquake. Real earthquakes are short and broadband, so real damper benefits are smaller.
  • The damper tuning rule is exact only for a one-storey building with no damping and a force on the main mass.
  • Epicentre: flat map, fixed depth, constant wave speeds (6.0 and 3.5 km/s by default). Distances on the flat map differ from great-circle distances by up to 4% at the corners of the map.
  • Arrival picks come up to about 1 s late, because the 1 s averaging window has to fill. A station nearer than about 55 km gives no distance: the S wave follows the P wave within 7 s and the two cannot be timed separately. For a station just beyond that the distance reads up to 6 km long.
  • At a trigger threshold of 2 or 3 the background noise alone crosses it, so the first "arrival" is often a false trigger. The station table marks these.
  • Jacobi, Gauss–Seidel, SOR, false position and fixed-point iteration stop when their last step is small. That is not the same as a small error: when they converge slowly the true error can be hundreds of times larger. The Lab tab shows the true error beside each.
  • The damage check fits one overall stiffness factor. It cannot say which storey is damaged.
  • The roof damper's mass is a share of the first mode's moving (modal) mass, not of the whole building. That moving mass is about half of the total for the 8-storey preset and under a third for the 30-storey tower.
  • Warning times use the true distance from the earthquake to each city, not the located one. The recovered magnitude reads about 0.03 low, because the synthetic wave train never quite reaches its nominal height.
  • For wall and frame + core buildings the stiffness matrix is the inverse of a flexibility matrix, which is badly conditioned for many storeys. At 40 storeys the condition number is about 107 and the residual shown in the calculation trail is about 10−9, against 10−13 for a frame.
  • The drawings are sketches. The windows and the trace along the ground in the Building tab are decoration; only the sway, the colours and the numbers come from the solver. A roof damper moves several times further than the roof, so its travel is cut off at the edge of the drawing.

The data

What is synthetic or assumed

  • Building presets are illustrative, not real buildings.
  • Earthquake load values (SDS, SD1, R, Cd) are examples.
  • Seismograms are generated: noise, a P wave, then a larger S wave.
  • Magnitude uses the Hutton and Boore (1987) Southern California distance correction, and the synthetic amplitude is made from the same formula. It tests the pipeline, not the physics.
  • Stations: UOSS is a real Global Seismographic Network station at the University of Sharjah. The others are hypothetical stations placed at cities.
  • Regression sample rows are synthetic.
  • Slow-shaking bands (1.0–1.75 s and 2.9–4.0 s) are from a summary of a study of the 2005 Qeshm earthquake recorded in the UAE. Check the paper before quoting them.
  • The phone motion-sensor recording has not been tested on a phone.
